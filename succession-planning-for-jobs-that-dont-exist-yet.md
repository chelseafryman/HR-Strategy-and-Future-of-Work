# Are We Preparing Successors for Jobs That Won't Exist?

For decades, succession planning has answered a question: If this leader leaves tomorrow, who takes their place? It's a logical question, but it assumes something that may no longer be true. It assumes the job will still look largely the same when the successor is ready to step into it.

I'm not sure that's a safe assumption anymore.

AI is changing work faster than most orgs update benefits. Entire portions of roles are being automated, new responsibilities appear almost overnight, and capabilities that weren't even discussed a year ago are suddenly business priorities.

**So, that raises a different question: What if the position we're preparing someone to take on won't exist in its current form by the time they're ready for it?**

Traditional succession planning focuses on matching people to jobs. We identify high-potential employees, assess them against competency models, and determine whether they're ready now or in a few years. There's nothing wrong with that process. The challenge is that the role itself is becoming a moving target.

Think about a Director of Operations. Five years ago, success may have depended on managing people, budgets, and production. Today, that same leader may also be expected to understand AI-assisted forecasting, workflow automation, predictive analytics, and how to redesign work so people and AI complement one another. Three years from now, the role may look different again.

If we're developing someone for yesterday's version of leadership, are we actually preparing them for tomorrow?

**I think succession planning needs to shift from role readiness to adaptability readiness.** We've been asking 'Can this person do this job?' when we should also be asking 'Can this person keep learning as the job evolves?'

That changes what we value in future leaders. Curiosity, learning agility, systems thinking, sound judgment, and the ability to lead through continuous change may become more important than mastering today's responsibilities. Those aren't simply leadership traits anymore. They may become competitive advantages.

It also changes HR's role. Instead of building succession plans around who fits today's org chart, we should be helping leaders think differently about the future. How is this role likely to change? What will it require that it doesn't today? If we were hiring for this position in 2030, what capabilities would we be looking for?

Those conversations don't just improve succession planning. They improve org design.

For years, succession planning has largely been about replacing people. I wonder if the future is less about preparing someone for a specific role and more about preparing adaptable leaders who can succeed in roles that don't exist yet.

**Because the biggest risk may not be having no successor. It may be successfully preparing someone for a job that no longer exists.**

If the roles you're planning for today look different in 18 months, what does that mean for how you're developing leaders right now?
