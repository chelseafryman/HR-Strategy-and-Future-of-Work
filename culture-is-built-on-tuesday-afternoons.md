# Culture Is Built on Tuesday Afternoons

I heard a line on Mike Rowe’s *The Way I Heard It* this week that has been stuck in my head:

**“Culture is not a microwavable event. It takes a crock pot approach.”**

The quote comes from Charlie Malouf of Broad River Retail, an Ashley Furniture licensee, and Garry Ridge, former CEO of WD-40, shared it during the conversation.

Ridge followed it with another line that may be even better: **“You can’t sprinkle fairy dust on an organization and change the culture.”**

Both made me think about how often organizations try to microwave culture.

Engagement scores drop, so we launch an initiative. Trust is low, so we hold a town hall. Employees say they don’t feel recognized, so we roll out a recognition program. Leaders aren't communicating well, so we schedule communication training.

None of those things are necessarily wrong. But an initiative can support a culture. It can't create one.

Culture is being built in all the ordinary moments between the initiatives. It’s what happens when someone makes a mistake. How a leader reacts when an employee disagrees with them. Whether people get the same answer when the answer is inconvenient. Who gets promoted. What behavior gets tolerated from a high performer. Whether leaders follow through on what they said six months ago when nobody is paying attention anymore.

*That's the crock pot part.*

Ridge talks about consistency as one of the ingredients. In another conversation, he described the work as knowing the ingredients of the culture you want and then working at them “consistently, consistently, consistently.”

I think that may be where culture efforts sometimes go sideways. We spend a lot of energy asking, “What should we do to improve our culture?”

Maybe a better question is: **“What are people experiencing from us every day that is teaching them what our culture actually is?”**

Because employees probably aren't defining the culture by what was announced at the last town hall.

**They're defining it by what happens on Tuesday afternoon when nobody is talking about culture.**
