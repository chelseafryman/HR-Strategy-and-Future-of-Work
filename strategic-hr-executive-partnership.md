# Strategic HR Isn't About Bringing Everything to the Table

There's been a lot of conversation over the years about HR earning a seat at the table.

But I think one of the things that actually earns credibility with executives is knowing what doesn't need to come to the table at all.

Not every employee issue needs executive attention. A policy decision doesn't always require leadership debate. Some risks can be managed without escalation, and not every HR initiative needs executive sponsorship. Part of being a strategic HR leader is knowing the difference.

**That also means knowing your executive team.** Some leaders want visibility early, even if they don't need to be involved. Others want you to manage the issue and bring them in when a decision or meaningful risk emerges. Good partnership means understanding where they want to be involved, keeping them informed, and using judgment about when something needs their attention.

Earlier in my career, I probably brought leaders more information than they needed. I thought being a strong partner meant making sure they knew everything. Experience taught me something different.

I remember working through a complicated employee situation with potential risk attached to it. I could have escalated every development. Instead, I kept the right people informed, worked through the issue at the appropriate level, and waited until there was a decision requiring executive involvement.

When I brought it forward, I wasn't bringing a running history of the problem. I was bringing the issue that mattered, the business implications, the options, and my recommendation.

**Executive visibility and executive involvement aren't the same thing.**

The executive team's attention is a limited resource. Every hour spent discussing something that could have been resolved elsewhere is an hour they aren't spending on customers, growth, operations, strategy, or issues only they can solve.

That doesn't mean HR operates independently of the business. Quite the opposite.

It means HR understands the business well enough to recognize when an issue has implications beyond HR.

Does it materially affect business performance? Does it create meaningful enterprise risk? Does it require a decision only this leadership team can make? Does it change something fundamental about how the organization operates?

If yes, bring it forward. And when you do, bring context, options, implications, and a recommendation, not simply a problem for the executive team to solve.

If not, it should probably be handled at the appropriate level without consuming executive attention.

I've found that executive partnership isn't built by being involved in every conversation or escalating every concern. It's built through judgment. Leaders begin to trust that when HR brings something to them, there's a reason it's there.

Maybe earning a seat at the table isn't really about getting invited into more conversations.

**It's about making the conversations you're part of more valuable.**
