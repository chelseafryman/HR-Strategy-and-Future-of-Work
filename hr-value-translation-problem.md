# HR Doesn't Have a Value Problem. It Has a Translation Problem.

One of the biggest opportunities for HR leaders isn't doing more strategic work. It's getting better at connecting the work we do to the financial outcomes the business cares about.

**Executives rarely question whether HR adds value. They question whether they can see the value.**

Too often we say:

- We improved engagement.
- We coached leaders.
- We reduced turnover.
- We redesigned onboarding.

Those are all meaningful accomplishments, but they don't explain the business impact.

**Instead, translate the outcome.**

Reducing turnover isn't just an HR win. Imagine a 500-person company with a 20% annual turnover rate. That's 100 employees leaving each year. If HR initiatives reduce turnover by just four percentage points, from 20% to 16%, that's 20 fewer employees to replace. Assuming the average cost to recruit, onboard, train, and absorb lost productivity is around $40,000 per employee, the company avoids roughly $800,000 in costs. That's before factoring in stronger customer relationships, less overtime, higher team productivity, and managers spending less time hiring and more time running the business.

**That's an EBITDA (bottom line) story executives immediately understand.**

Every HR initiative should answer at least one of four questions:

- Did we increase revenue?
- Did we reduce costs?
- Did we reduce risk?
- Did we improve the organization's ability to execute?

Those are the levers that move EBITDA.

HR doesn't need to become finance. We simply need to become more fluent in the language of business. When we connect people strategies to financial outcomes, leaders stop seeing HR as a support function and start seeing it as a driver of business performance.
