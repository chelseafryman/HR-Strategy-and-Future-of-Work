# What If AI Creates Capacity, Not Just Reduces Headcount?

We may be asking the wrong question about AI and the future of work.

AI workforce planning shouldn't begin with jobs that are "at risk." It should begin with work that is changing.

Imagine you have (an easy number) 100 employees performing the same role and determine that AI can automate or significantly reduce 25% of the work they do.

The easy math is: 100 employees × 25% automation = 25 fewer employees. **We need leaders to STOP thinking like this as a first thought!**

What if we looked at that 25% as *CAPACITY* instead?

Before deciding we need fewer people, what higher-value work could those employees now do? What work have we historically said we don't have time for? Where could we provide deeper analysis instead of reporting data? Where could employees spend more time with customers, solving problems, improving processes or anticipating issues instead of reacting to them? What capabilities could we build that weren't realistic when people were spending 25% of their time on work AI can now handle?

The answer may still be that the organization needs fewer people. But it could also mean we need people doing different work, developing different skills, or producing significantly more value than the role was originally designed to produce.

We've seen versions of this before. Spreadsheets made financial calculations dramatically faster, but we didn't simply decide we needed fewer analysts. The expectation of what analysis could provide changed. Technology created capacity, and that capacity helped create more sophisticated work.

AI gives us an opportunity to rethink roles in much the same way, except this time the impact could reach almost every function.

There's also a human side to this. **If employees believe that every five hours they save using AI makes them five hours more expendable, we shouldn't be surprised when AI adoption becomes difficult.**

Realistically, some jobs will disappear and some organizations will need fewer people. That's part of this transition. But headcount shouldn't automatically be the first place we go when AI creates capacity.

Maybe the better future of work question isn't "How many jobs can AI replace?" Maybe it's "If AI gives us 25% of our capacity back, what could our organization do that we don't have the capacity (time) to do today?"
