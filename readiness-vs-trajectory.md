# Are We Overvaluing Readiness and Undervaluing Trajectory?

Imagine two potential successors. Sarah knows the business inside and out, has spent years in the function, and could step into the VP role tomorrow with minimal disruption. Anna isn't quite as ready today. She's worked across different functions, taken on unfamiliar problems, helped integrate an acquisition, and repeatedly shown she can get up to speed fast when circumstances change.

In a stable business, Sarah may look like the obvious choice. But if you know the business is going to double in size, expand globally, or fundamentally change its operating model over the next three years, **who actually has the stronger succession profile?**

One of my previous posts asked whether we're preparing people for roles that may no longer exist. This one asks something related but different: **Are we overlooking our best future leaders because they aren't the most "ready" today?**

Readiness feels safer. A leader who already looks like the current VP is easy to defend in a talent review. The person with greater trajectory requires the organization to make a bet. But succession planning is, by definition, a bet on the future. Our processes naturally favor the evidence we've already seen over the potential we haven't yet seen.

So maybe the conversation shouldn't stop at Ready Now, Ready in 6-12 months, Ready in 12-24 months. Ask instead: What has this person learned that they didn't already know? How have they responded when the business changed underneath them?

**The person most ready for today's job and the person with the greatest potential for tomorrow's job may not always be the same person.**

If they aren't, *which one is your succession process more likely to choose?*
